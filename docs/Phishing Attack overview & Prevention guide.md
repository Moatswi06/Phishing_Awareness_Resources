# PHISHING
*A form of cyber attack where by criminals trick victims into giving away sensitive information, which includes passwords, credit card number also personal IDs. They do this by pretending to be a trusted source such as banks, a coworker a popular online service and people fall for this as they to tend them easily without doing proper research and sometimes they fall for this due to pressure applied to them by criminals with gives them less time to think and they take decisions under pressure.*

1.Social Engineering Techniques
**MFA fatigue** - flooding a victim with approval prompts until they click accept as they get annoyed.

**Deepfakes** - Artificial Intelligence generated videos and audios of someone familiar asking for urgently which may make them give out money easily.

**Romance scams** - A type of confidence scheme where a cybercriminal creates a fake online persona to gain a victims affection and trust.

# Types of Phishing attacks

**Email Phishing:** Criminals use this method by sending fake emails pretending to be trusted sources/organizations to steal sensitive information, they often request login credentials, banking details and personal IDs. Example: An email claiming to be from a bank telling customers to click on the link to update their KYC.

**Spear Phishing:** A form of phishing attack aimed at a specific individual or an organization. Criminals use professional information to appear legitimate, Example: An example sent to an employee appearing to be from the manager request for access to confidential files.

**Smishing:**A form of phishing where criminals send fake SMS or text messages which contain fraudulent links or fake alerts that steal credentials and OTPs. Example: A text sent to a victim message calming they won a sum of money from a competition and they should press the link to verify their identity.


# How to recognize phishing

**Urgency:** The messages always have sense of urgency and they mostly instills fear in people or panic and they tend to build pressure which lead to bad decision making.

**Unexpected requests:** The messages ask for passwords or payments details out of nowhere usually sensitive information is stored in the bank's system and they never ask for it through phone calls.

**To good to be true:** The messages offer prize money that is way too much and sometimes they mention competitions an individual never entered.
